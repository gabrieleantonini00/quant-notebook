Dynamic panels and GMM

A lagged outcome can describe persistence, but in a short panel it also changes the identification problem. Removing unit effects does not remove the correlation induced by the lagged dependent variable.

The dynamic panel equation

$$y_{it}=\rho y_{i,t-1}+x_{it}^{\mathsf T}\beta+c_i+\varepsilon_{it}.$$

The parameter $\rho$ measures persistence conditional on the included regressors and unit effect. The usual micro-panel setting has many units and few dates. Pooled OLS generally correlates the lagged outcome with $c_i$; fixed effects remove $c_i$ but introduce correlation between the demeaned lag and the demeaned disturbance.

Past outcomes contain past errors. The within error subtracts an average that includes those same errors. In the basic AR(1) setting, the resulting Nickell bias is typically downward and of order $1/T$. Increasing the number of units with fixed $T$ does not eliminate it. Its magnitude and even simple sign intuitions should not be transferred mechanically to arbitrary richer specifications.

Why differencing is not enough

$$\Delta y_{it}=\rho\Delta y_{i,t-1}+\Delta x_{it}^{\mathsf T}\beta+\Delta\varepsilon_{it}.$$

The unit effect disappears, but $\Delta y_{i,t-1}$ includes $\varepsilon_{i,t-1}$, while the differenced disturbance includes $-\varepsilon_{i,t-1}$. Under the basic serially uncorrelated innovation assumptions,

$$\operatorname{Cov}(\Delta y_{i,t-1},\Delta\varepsilon_{it})=-\sigma_\varepsilon^2\ne0.$$

OLS on the differenced equation is therefore generally inconsistent. This is an identification problem; changing standard errors alone cannot solve it.

Use timing restrictions to build instruments

With serially uncorrelated levels innovations and compatible initial-condition restrictions, outcomes dated $t-2$ and earlier can be orthogonal to $\Delta\varepsilon_{it}$:

$$E[y_{is}(\varepsilon_{it}-\varepsilon_{i,t-1})]=0,\qquad s\le t-2.$$

For example, at $t=3$, $y_{i1}$ is a candidate instrument for $\Delta y_{i2}$; at $t=4$, candidates include $y_{i1}$ and $y_{i2}$. They must also predict the endogenous differenced regressor sufficiently well. These conditions are assumptions about the economic process, not consequences of writing dates as subscripts.

Regressor timingMeaning relative to levels innovationsCandidate level instruments for the differenced equation
Strictly exogenousOrthogonal to past, current, and future innovationsAll dates can qualify under the maintained restrictions

More persistent error processes can invalidate these lag windows. If a unit has missing dates, “two observations ago” is not necessarily two calendar periods ago.

Difference GMM combines moment conditions

Let $Z_i$ contain the chosen instruments and $v_i(\delta)$ the vector of differenced residuals for parameter vector $\delta=(\rho,\beta^{\mathsf T})^{\mathsf T}$. Difference GMM minimizes

$$g_N(\delta)=\frac1N\sum_iZ_i^{\mathsf T}v_i(\delta),\qquad \hat\delta=\arg\min_\delta g_N(\delta)^{\mathsf T}A_Ng_N(\delta).$$

A one-step weighting matrix uses a maintained error structure. Two-step GMM estimates the moment covariance using preliminary residuals and updates the weighting matrix. For independent units, the central covariance estimate is built from the unit-level moment vectors, preserving dependence across dates inside a unit.

Two-step standard errors can be downward biased in finite samples. A Windmeijer-type correction addresses an important part of that problem; it does not guarantee valid inference in every sample or fix invalid instruments.

System GMM adds assumptions, not just equations

When $y$ is highly persistent, lagged levels can be weak predictors of changes. System GMM combines differenced equations with equations in levels. Lagged differences can instrument the levels equation under additional restrictions such as

$$E[\Delta y_{i,t-1}(c_i+\varepsilon_{it})]=0.$$

This requires an assumption connecting initial conditions or changes to the unit effect; it does not follow merely from no serial correlation of $\varepsilon_{it}$. Mean-stationarity conditions are one route. System GMM can improve performance when those extra moments are valid and informative, but it is not an automatic cure for persistence or weak identification.

Using every available lag can rapidly proliferate instruments, overfit the endogenous regressors, and weaken specification tests. Report $N$, observed time spans, instrument count, timing classifications, lag limits, one- or two-step estimation, covariance correction, and diagnostic results. Instrument count below $N$ is a useful warning heuristic, not a sufficient validity condition.

If the aim is a long-run multiplier, $\beta_k/(1-\rho)$ requires a stable scalar dynamic relationship, $|\rho|<1$, and appropriate interpretation of the regressor path. For illustrative $\rho=0.6$ and $\beta_k=0.8$, the sustained-change multiplier is 2; uncertainty in both estimates must be propagated.
