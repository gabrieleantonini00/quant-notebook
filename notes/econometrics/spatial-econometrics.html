Spatial Econometrics

Spatial Weights

A spatial weights matrix $W\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}$ specifies connections among $n$ locations. Entry $w_{ij}$ weights the influence of location $j$ on location $i$, usually with $w_{ii}=0$. Connections can reflect shared borders, distance or a substantive network.

Given nonnegative connection weights $g_{ij}$, row standardisation sets

$$\begin{aligned}w_{ij}&=\frac{g_{ij}}{\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}g_{i\ell}},\\(Wy)_i&=\sum_{j=1}^{n}w_{ij}y_j.\end{aligned}$$

For a location with neighbours, the row sums to one and its spatial lag is a weighted average of neighbouring outcomes. Isolated locations need an explicit convention, often a zero row. Row standardisation can make $W$ asymmetric even when the underlying connections are undirected.

$W^m$ describes connections through $m$ network steps. Although $w_{ii}=0$, $(W^2)_{ii}$ can be positive because a path can leave a location and return. These are network steps, not time periods. The choice and normalisation of $W$ are part of the specification and should reflect the mechanism being studied.

Outcome Dependence (SAR)

The spatial autoregressive model links each outcome to neighbouring outcomes:

$$\begin{aligned}y&=\rho Wy+X\beta+u,\\y&=(I-\rho W)^{-1}(X\beta+u).\end{aligned}$$

$X$ contains local covariates, $\rho$ measures outcome interaction and $u$ is the disturbance. The reduced form requires $I-\rho W$ to be invertible. When $|\rho|r(W)<1$, where $r(W)$ is the spectral radius,

$$(I-\rho W)^{-1}=I+\rho W+\rho^2W^2+\cdots.$$

The multiplier propagates a change through immediate neighbours, more distant locations and paths returning to its origin. For nonnegative row-standardised $W$, $|\rho|<1$ is sufficient for convergence. This bound depends on the normalisation of $W$.

Covariate Spillovers (SLX)

The spatially lagged X model includes neighbouring characteristics directly:

$$y=X\beta+WX\theta+u.$$

For covariate $k$, $\beta_k$ measures the local response and $\theta_k w_{ij}$ the response in location $i$ to a change in location $j$. There is no outcome feedback: the effect matrix is $\beta_k I+\theta_k W$. With exogenous regressors and suitable regularity conditions, OLS can estimate this model.

In SLX and SDM, $WX\theta$ conventionally contains only lagged nonconstant covariates. With row sums equal to one, a lagged intercept duplicates the intercept.

Error Dependence (SEM)

The spatial error model places dependence in unobserved shocks:

$$\begin{aligned}y&=X\beta+u,\\u&=\lambda Wu+\varepsilon,\\u&=(I-\lambda W)^{-1}\varepsilon.\end{aligned}$$

$\lambda$ controls error propagation. If $E[\varepsilon\mid X,W]=0$ and $\operatorname{Var}(\varepsilon\mid X,W)=\sigma^2 I$, then

$$\operatorname{Var}(u\mid X,W)=\sigma^2(I-\lambda W)^{-1}(I-\lambda W)^{-\mathsf T}.$$

Spatially correlated errors do not by themselves imply covariate spillovers in the conditional mean. Under exogeneity and suitable regularity conditions, OLS coefficients can remain consistent, but conventional OLS standard errors ignore the error dependence and OLS is generally inefficient. Spatial-error likelihood or GMM estimation accounts for this structure.

Combined Dependence (SDM)

The spatial Durbin model combines outcome interaction and neighbouring characteristics:

$$y=\rho Wy+X\beta+WX\theta+u.$$

Setting $\theta=0$ gives SAR; setting $\rho=0$ gives SLX. With $W\boldsymbol{1}=\boldsymbol{1}$ and an invertible multiplier, the common-factor restriction $\theta=-\rho\beta$ on nonconstant covariates gives an SEM representation with $\lambda=\rho$. An SDM intercept $a$ then becomes $a/(1-\rho)$ in the SEM representation.

Adding $WX$ allows covariate spillovers to differ from the propagation induced by $Wy$. It does not automatically resolve omitted-variable bias or endogeneity.

Direct & Indirect Effects

With $E[u\mid X,W]=0$ and fixed $W$, the SDM marginal-effect matrix for covariate $k$ is

$$\begin{aligned}S_k&=\frac{\partial E[y\mid X,W]}{\partial x_k^{\mathsf T}}\\&=(I-\rho W)^{-1}(\beta_k I+\theta_k W).\end{aligned}$$

Entry $(S_k)_{ij}$ measures the response in location $i$ to a change in location $j$. Diagonal entries are direct effects, including feedback returning to the origin; off-diagonal entries are indirect effects, or spillovers. SAR uses $\theta_k=0$.

$$\begin{aligned}\mathrm{ADE}_k&=\frac{\operatorname{tr}(S_k)}{n},\\ \mathrm{ATE}_k&=\frac{\boldsymbol{1}^{\mathsf T}S_k\boldsymbol{1}}{n},\\ \mathrm{AIE}_k&=\mathrm{ATE}_k-\mathrm{ADE}_k.\end{aligned}$$

These are the average direct, total and indirect effects. A coefficient $\beta_k$ alone generally does not equal the direct effect once outcome feedback is present.

For this symmetric network, ADE is 1.095, AIE is 0.571 and ATE is 1.667. Interpreting these responses causally requires identification assumptions beyond the spatial specification.

Estimation

In SAR and SDM, $Wy$ is generally endogenous: neighbouring outcomes respond to disturbances in the same simultaneous system. OLS on the structural equation is therefore generally inconsistent.

Maximum Likelihood

For SAR with $u\sim N(0,\sigma^2 I)$, define $u(\rho,\beta)=(I-\rho W)y-X\beta$. Conditional on $X$ and $W$, the log-likelihood is

$$\begin{aligned}\ell&=\log|\det(I-\rho W)|\\&\quad-\frac n2\log(2\pi\sigma^2)-\frac{u^{\mathsf T}u}{2\sigma^2}.\end{aligned}$$

The log-determinant is the Jacobian of the transformation from outcomes to innovations. It cannot be dropped when estimating $\rho$. For SDM, the residual also subtracts $WX\theta$.

Instrumental Variables

$WX$ and $W^2X$ can provide instruments for $Wy$ when they are exogenous, relevant and satisfy the exclusion restrictions. In SDM, included $WX$ columns are regressors, so those same columns cannot serve as excluded instruments. Higher-order spatial lags still need a defensible exclusion argument and sufficient strength.

Geographical weights are not automatically exogenous. Check sensitivity to plausible neighbour definitions, normalisation and isolated locations. Residual spatial correlation can flag a misspecification, but alone does not distinguish outcome dependence from covariate spillovers or correlated shocks.
