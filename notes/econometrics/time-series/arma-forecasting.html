ARMA & Forecasting

AR, MA & ARMA Models

An ARMA($p,q$) combines lagged observations and innovations. With the plus-sign convention for MA coefficients,

$$\begin{aligned}y_t&=c+\sum_{j=1}^{p}\phi_jy_{t-j}+\varepsilon_t\\&\quad+\sum_{j=1}^{q}\theta_j\varepsilon_{t-j}.\end{aligned}$$

$\varepsilon_t$ is white noise with variance $\sigma^2$. AR terms transmit dependence through past outcomes; MA terms use past innovations. A finite MA with finite-variance white noise is covariance-stationary. Invertibility is a separate condition allowing innovations to be recovered from observed history.

AR(1)

$$y_t=c+\phi y_{t-1}+\varepsilon_t,\qquad |\phi|<1.$$

For the causal stationary solution,

$$\begin{aligned}\mu&=\frac{c}{1-\phi},\qquad \operatorname{Var}(y_t)=\frac{\sigma^2}{1-\phi^2},\\y_t-\mu&=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\phi^j\varepsilon_{t-j},\qquad \rho(k)=\phi^{|k|}.\end{aligned}$$

Positive $\phi$ gives persistence; negative $\phi$ gives alternating responses. Shocks decay geometrically in either case.

MA(1)

$$y_t=\mu+\varepsilon_t+\theta\varepsilon_{t-1}.$$

Its variance is $\sigma^2(1+\theta^2)$, with $\rho(1)=\theta/(1+\theta^2)$ and $\rho(k)=0$ for $|k|>1$. It is invertible when $|\theta|<1$.

Stationarity & Invertibility

The lag operator satisfies $Ly_t=y_{t-1}$. Write $\phi(L)y_t=c+\theta(L)\varepsilon_t$, where

$$\begin{aligned}\phi(z)&=1-\phi_1z-\cdots-\phi_pz^p,\\\theta(z)&=1+\theta_1z+\cdots+\theta_qz^q.\end{aligned}$$

After cancelling common factors, a causal stationary ARMA has AR polynomial roots outside the unit circle; invertibility requires MA polynomial roots outside it. Companion eigenvalues use the reciprocal convention and lie inside the unit circle for stability: $z=1/\lambda$.

For $y_t=y_{t-1}-0.5y_{t-2}+\varepsilon_t$, companion eigenvalues $(1\pm i)/2$ have modulus $1/\sqrt2$, while lag roots $1\pm i$ have modulus $\sqrt2$. Both describe stable, damped oscillations.

Wold's decomposition gives an infinite linear innovation representation for a purely nondeterministic covariance-stationary process. It does not imply an exact finite ARMA representation or equality between the best linear forecast and every nonlinear conditional expectation.

ACF & PACF

The ACF measures correlation across a lag. The PACF at lag $k$ removes the linear contribution of intervening lags; it equals the last coefficient in the population linear projection on the first $k$ lags.

AR($p$)Tails offCuts off after $p$
MA($q$)Cuts off after $q$Tails off

Sample ACF and PACF patterns are noisy. Nearly cancelling dynamics and mixed models can obscure these population rules.

Estimation & Diagnostics

Stable AR models can be estimated by OLS under appropriate innovation assumptions. Lagged outcomes generally preclude finite-sample strict exogeneity, so consistency does not imply exact unbiasedness. MA and ARMA models typically use conditional or exact likelihood.

$$\begin{aligned}\mathrm{AIC}&=-2\widehat\ell+2k,\\\mathrm{BIC}&=-2\widehat\ell+k\log T.\end{aligned}$$

Compare criteria on the same response and comparable likelihood sample, counting all estimated parameters consistently. Integration order is a prior specification choice, not a competition in levels-regression $R^2$.

Residual autocorrelation can be assessed with the Ljung–Box statistic:

$$Q=T(T+2)\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{\widehat\rho_j^2}{T-j}.$$

Fitted ARMA diagnostics commonly use an approximate $\chi^2_{m-p-q}$ reference with $m>p+q$, subject to specification and regularity conditions. Conditional heteroskedasticity can affect calibration; zero residual autocorrelation does not imply independence.

Forecasting

Under conditional-mean-zero innovations, set future innovations to zero and substitute earlier forecasts for unobserved lagged outcomes. For AR(1),

$$\begin{aligned}\widehat y_{t+h\mid t}&=\mu+\phi^h(y_t-\mu),\\v_h&=\sigma^2\sum_{j=0}^{h-1}\phi^{2j}.\end{aligned}$$

$v_h$ is the conditional forecast-error variance with known parameters and homoskedastic innovations. It approaches the unconditional variance as the forecast returns to $\mu$. Under Gaussian innovations, pointwise prediction limits are $\widehat y_{t+h\mid t}\pm1.96\sqrt{v_h}$.

Non-Gaussian innovations require suitable distributional quantiles or simulation. Parameter estimation and model selection add uncertainty beyond the displayed innovation variance.

ARIMA Models

$$\phi(L)(1-L)^d y_t=c+\theta(L)\varepsilon_t.$$

ARIMA($p,d,q$) applies an ARMA model after $d$ differences. A constant in a first-difference equation can imply drift in levels. Reconstruct level forecasts and their uncertainty through the integration step. Seasonal AR and MA terms, and seasonal differences, require the corresponding seasonal lag polynomials.

Forecast Evaluation

Evaluate at rolling or expanding origins using only information available at each date. Refit parameters, transformations and scaling inside each training window. Choose tuning parameters without using the future evaluation sample.

State the forecast horizon and compare a relevant benchmark. Across $N$ evaluation errors $e_j$ at that horizon,

$$\begin{aligned}\mathrm{MAE}&=\frac1N\sum_{j=1}^{N}|e_j|,\\\mathrm{RMSE}&=\sqrt{\frac1N\sum_{j=1}^{N}e_j^2}.\end{aligned}$$

A random walk can benchmark price levels; a historical mean can benchmark returns. Overlapping forecast errors remain dependent, which matters for uncertainty in comparisons.
