VAR, Cointegration & VECM

Vector Autoregression

A VAR($p$) models an $m$-dimensional vector $y_t$ using lags of all included outcomes:

$$y_t=c+\sum_{j=1}^{p}A_jy_{t-j}+Bx_t+\varepsilon_t.$$

Each $A_j$ is $m\times m$. $Bx_t$ collects optional deterministic or exogenous terms. Innovations have zero conditional mean given past and included exogenous information; their contemporaneous covariance matrix $\Sigma$ need not be diagonal.

For an illustrative VAR(1) in inflation $\pi_t$ and the policy interest rate $i_t$,

$$\begin{aligned}\pi_t&=c_\pi+a_{11}\pi_{t-1}+a_{12}i_{t-1}+\varepsilon_{\pi,t},\\i_t&=c_i+a_{21}\pi_{t-1}+a_{22}i_{t-1}+\varepsilon_{i,t}.\end{aligned}$$

A stable causal VAR has companion eigenvalues inside the unit circle, equivalently roots of $\det(I-A_1z-\cdots-A_pz^p)=0$ outside it. Under a stationary, correctly specified model with valid regressors, equation-by-equation OLS estimates the reduced form. With identical regressors in every equation, SUR gives the same coefficients.

Choose lags on a common sample, allowing for the system's parameter count. Check residual serial correlation, stability and forecast performance. Correcting a covariance estimate cannot repair omitted dynamics that make lagged regressors endogenous.

Granger Predictability

Variable $j$ does not Granger-cause variable $i$ relative to the information set when its lags add no predictive content. In a linear VAR, test the joint restrictions

$$H_0:(A_1)_{ij}=\cdots=(A_p)_{ij}=0.$$

Rejecting this null establishes predictive content under the maintained model. It does not identify an intervention effect: common information, omitted variables and anticipation can produce predictive ordering. Standard test calibration also depends on integration and cointegration.

Impulse Responses

For a stable VAR with constant mean and no time-varying exogenous terms, the innovation representation is

$$\begin{aligned}y_t-\mu&=\sum_{h=0}^{\infty}\Psi_h\varepsilon_{t-h},\\\Psi_0&=I,\qquad\Psi_h=\sum_{j=1}^{\min(p,h)}A_j\Psi_{h-j}.\end{aligned}$$

Column $j$ of $\Psi_h$ gives the response to a unit reduced-form innovation in coordinate $j$, holding the other innovation coordinates fixed. When innovations are correlated, this is not yet an isolated structural shock.

Writing $\varepsilon_t=Pu_t$ with $\operatorname{Var}(u_t)=I$ gives structural responses $\Psi_hP$. The condition $PP^{\mathsf T}=\Sigma$ does not uniquely determine $P$. A Cholesky decomposition imposes recursive contemporaneous restrictions, so the ordering matters. Signs, long-run restrictions or external instruments require their own identifying assumptions.

Cointegration

If the components of $y_t$ are $I(1)$ but a nonzero vector $\beta$ gives $\beta^{\mathsf T}y_t\sim I(0)$, the variables are cointegrated. They share stochastic trends while one or more linear combinations remain stationary.

For an illustrative consumption–income relationship, suppose $C_t$ and disposable income $Y_t$ are $I(1)$ but

$$z_t=C_t-0.8Y_t\sim I(0).$$

Consumption need not equal 80% of income at every date. The deviation $z_t$ fluctuates around a stable mean. The coefficient 0.8 is illustrative; intercepts or trends in the relation change its specification.

A high levels-regression $R^2$ is insufficient. The defining property is stationarity of the long-run combination. Differencing all variables and omitting that combination discards long-run information.

Error Correction (VECM)

Subtracting $y_{t-1}$ from a VAR($p$) and collecting differences gives, with deterministic terms omitted for notation,

$$\Delta y_t=\Pi y_{t-1}+\sum_{j=1}^{p-1}\Gamma_j\Delta y_{t-j}+\varepsilon_t.$$
$$\begin{aligned}\Pi&=\sum_{j=1}^{p}A_j-I,\\\Gamma_j&=-\sum_{k=j+1}^{p}A_k.\end{aligned}$$

For VAR(2), substitute $y_{t-2}=y_{t-1}-\Delta y_{t-1}$: $\Pi=A_1+A_2-I$ and $\Gamma_1=-A_2$. This reparameterisation is algebraic. Cointegration adds the reduced-rank restriction

$$\Pi=\alpha\beta^{\mathsf T},\qquad\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb R^{m\times r},\quad0<r<m.$$

The columns of $\beta$ define stationary equilibrium errors; $\alpha$ gives each equation's response to them. Short-run dynamics enter through the $\Gamma_j$. The individual factors require a normalisation: replacing $\beta$ by $-\beta$ also reverses $\alpha$ without changing $\Pi$.

Consumption–Income Adjustment

For $z_{t-1}=C_{t-1}-0.8Y_{t-1}$, the error-correction contributions are

$$\begin{aligned}\Delta C_t&=\alpha_C z_{t-1}+\cdots,\\\Delta Y_t&=\alpha_Y z_{t-1}+\cdots.\end{aligned}$$

With only this adjustment and zero-mean innovations,

$$E[z_t\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]=(1+\alpha_C-0.8\alpha_Y)z_{t-1}.$$

The simplified gap is stable if $|1+\alpha_C-0.8\alpha_Y|<1$. An individual adjustment coefficient is not the whole system's percentage speed of correction. In a full VECM, stability also depends on short-run dynamics.

Cointegration Rank & Testing

Under the maintained non-explosive $I(0)/I(1)$ setup:

Rank of $\Pi$ImplicationRepresentation
$r=0$No cointegrating combinationVAR in stationary differences
$0<r<m$$r$ cointegrating relationsReduced-rank VECM
$r=m$Stationary levels under stability conditionsVAR in levels

Engle–Granger estimates an equilibrium relation and tests its residuals for a unit root. The test needs cointegration-specific critical values because the residuals use estimated coefficients; ordinary ADF critical values do not apply.

Johansen tests assess rank within a system. Lag order and deterministic terms, including whether constants or trends lie inside or outside the cointegrating relations, affect the model and reference distributions. Rank interpretation excludes explosive and higher-order integrated cases; breaks and small samples can alter the evidence.
