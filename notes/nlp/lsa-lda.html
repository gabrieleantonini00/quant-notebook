LSA & LDA

Topic modeling and dimensionality reduction: from linear-algebraic Singular Value Decomposition (LSA) to Bayesian generative priors and Markov Chain Monte Carlo inference (LDA).

Latent Semantic Analysis (LSA)

Latent Semantic Analysis (LSA) relies on the mathematical principles of Singular Value Decomposition (SVD), closely related to Principal Component Analysis (PCA) used heavily in econometrics for dimensionality reduction and latent variable extraction.

Mathematical Formulation

Given a sparse Term-Document matrix $A \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times M}$ (where entries represent word frequencies or TF-IDF scores), SVD factors $A$ into the product of three matrices:

$$A \approx U_k \Sigma_k V_k^T$$

where rank-$k$ truncation retains the top $k$ principal dimensions:

  • $U_k \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times k}$ (Left Singular Vectors):
    Crucial Insight: Embeddings vs. Topics
    • Rows (Early Embeddings): Each row provides a dense numeric vector for a single word. This is the direct linear-algebra ancestor of modern word embeddings (like Word2Vec), enabling geometric similarity computation (e.g. Cosine Similarity).
    • Columns (Latent Directions): Each column is a latent direction, analogous to a principal component. Because SVD permits negative weights (e.g., $+0.6\,\text{bank} - 0.3\,\text{river}$), a direction can contrast word groups rather than describe a probability distribution. Some directions are interpretable, but that interpretation is not guaranteed.
  • $\Sigma_k \in \mathbb{R}^{k \times k}$ (Singular Values): Diagonal matrix with $\sigma_i=\sqrt{\lambda_i}$, where $\lambda_i$ is an eigenvalue of $A^T A$. The squared values quantify matrix energy; interpreting this as centered variance requires centering, which LSA usually does not do.
  • $V_k^T \in \mathbb{R}^{k \times M}$ (Right Singular Vectors): Describes document directions. A common choice of document coordinates is $\Sigma_k V_k^T=U_k^T A$; word coordinates can similarly use $U_k\Sigma_k$.

Gram Matrices (Covariance Analogues)

  • $A A^T$: Contains dot-product similarities between word rows across documents.
  • $A^T A$: Contains dot-product similarities between document columns (weighted overlap, not generally a count of shared words). Using the compact SVD:
    $$A^T A = (U \Sigma V^T)^T (U \Sigma V^T) = V \Sigma U^T U \Sigma V^T = V \Sigma^2 V^T$$

Latent Dirichlet Allocation (LDA)

Latent Dirichlet Allocation (LDA) is a fully Bayesian, probabilistic generative model. It treats documents as random mixtures over latent topics, and each topic as a discrete distribution over vocabulary words.

Core Random Variables & Parameters

$K$: Predefined number of latent topics
$\theta_d$: Topic distribution for document $d$ (point on simplex)
$\phi_k$: Word distribution for topic $k$ (point on simplex)
$\alpha, \beta$: Dirichlet hyperparameter priors (pseudocounts)

Generative Process

In the fully Bayesian formulation, the topic-word distributions are shared across the corpus:

  1. Once for each topic $k \in \{1, \dots, K\}$, sample a word distribution:
    $$\phi_k \sim \text{Dir}(\beta)$$
  2. For each document $d$, sample its topic distribution:
    $$\theta_d \sim \text{Dir}(\alpha)$$
  3. For each word position $n$ in document $d$:
    • Sample a latent topic assignment:
      $$z_{d,n} \sim \text{Categorical}(\theta_d)$$
    • Sample the observed word given the chosen topic:
      $$w_{d,n} \sim \text{Categorical}(\phi_{z_{d,n}})$$

Statistical Inference & Dirichlet Priors (MLE vs. MAP)

  • The Sparsity Problem: Given fixed topic assignments, a multinomial MLE gives zero probability to words with zero counts in a topic. This motivates smoothing, especially when topic-specific counts are small.
  • The Bayesian Smoothing Solution: Positive Dirichlet hyperparameters act as pseudocounts in posterior means and predictive probabilities. For known topic assignments, a word probability has posterior mean $(n_{k,w}+\beta_w)/(n_k+\sum_v\beta_v)$, which remains positive even when $n_{k,w}=0$.
  • Posterior Inference vs. MAP: A prior defines a posterior distribution; MAP is only one possible summary. A Dirichlet prior is not equivalent to the Gaussian prior underlying ridge regression. In particular, sparse Dirichlet priors can have boundary modes, so MAP does not guarantee strictly positive probabilities.
  • Intractable Posterior & Approximation: Because the exact denominator of the joint posterior $P(\boldsymbol{\theta}, \boldsymbol{\phi}, \mathbf{z} \mid \mathbf{w}, \alpha, \beta)$ involves summing over all possible topic combinations, exact inference is intractable. Approximation algorithms such as Collapsed Gibbs Sampling (MCMC) or Mean-Field Variational Inference are employed.

Worked example · one document, two topics

From latent directions to topic probabilities

Imagine a corpus where “cat” and “dog” often occur together, as do “bond” and “stock”. LSA can compress these co-occurrence patterns into signed directions. LDA represents them as nonnegative word probabilities. For illustration, suppose the fitted topics were:

Illustrative topic-word probabilities; each row sums to 1.
Topiccatdogbondstock
Pets0.450.450.050.05
Finance0.050.050.450.45

For a document with topic mixture $\theta_d=(0.75,0.25)$, marginalize over its two possible topics:

$$P(\text{cat}\mid d)=0.75(0.45)+0.25(0.05)=0.35$$

Key distinction: an LDA document can mix topics. Its 75% “Pets” weight is a probability, while an LSA coordinate is not. Topic names are interpretations supplied after fitting.
