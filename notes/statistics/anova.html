ANOVA

Model & Hypotheses

One-way ANOVA compares the population means of $k$ groups. For observation $j$ in group $i$, the model is $Y_{ij}=\mu_i+\varepsilon_{ij}$:

$$H_0:\mu_1=\cdots=\mu_k,\qquad H_1:\text{at least one mean differs}.$$

This is an omnibus test: rejection identifies an overall difference, without identifying which pairs differ.

Variance Decomposition

Let $n_i$ be the size of group $i$, $\bar y_i$ its sample mean, $N=\sum_i n_i$ and $\bar y$ the grand mean. The total squared variation separates into between-group and within-group components:

$$SS_{\mathrm{between}}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}n_i(\bar y_i-\bar y)^2,$$
$$SS_{\mathrm{within}}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{n_i}(y_{ij}-\bar y_i)^2,$$
$$SS_{\mathrm{total}}=SS_{\mathrm{between}}+SS_{\mathrm{within}}.$$

F Test

Divide each sum of squares by its degrees of freedom, then compare the two mean squares:

$$F=\frac{SS_{\mathrm{between}}/(k-1)}{SS_{\mathrm{within}}/(N-k)}=\frac{MS_{\mathrm{between}}}{MS_{\mathrm{within}}}.$$

Under the classical assumptions and $H_0$, $F\sim F_{k-1,N-k}$. Reject when $F>F_{k-1,N-k;1-\alpha}$. A large ratio indicates that group means differ more than within-group noise would explain.

Assumptions

The exact F test assumes independent observations, normal errors within groups and a common error variance. Welch's ANOVA relaxes the common-variance assumption. Repeated observations on the same unit require a model that accounts for their dependence.

Interpretation & Follow-up Comparisons

Rejection does not identify the differing pairs or quantify practical importance. For all pairwise comparisons under the classical model, Tukey's method accounts for the family of comparisons; planned contrasts can use an appropriate multiple-testing adjustment.

ANOVA alone does not establish causality; causal interpretation requires an appropriate design or identification strategy.
