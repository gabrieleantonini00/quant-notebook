ANOVA

Factors, Levels & Factorial Designs

A factor is a grouping variable; its categories or settings are its levels. The number of factors determines whether ANOVA is one-way, two-way or three-way. In a fully crossed factorial design, every combination of levels is represented by a cell.

A $2\times3$ design is therefore two-way ANOVA. With $r$ independent observations per cell, $N=6r$; the six cells are not the sample size. This notation specifies level counts, while independence or repeated measurement must be specified separately.

One-Way ANOVA

One-way ANOVA compares the population means of $k$ groups. For observation $j$ in group $i$, the model is $Y_{ij}=\mu_i+\varepsilon_{ij}$:

$$H_0:\mu_1=\cdots=\mu_k,\qquad H_1:\text{at least one mean differs}.$$

This is an omnibus test: rejection identifies an overall difference, without identifying which pairs differ.

Variance Decomposition

Let $n_i$ be the size of group $i$, $\bar y_i$ its sample mean, $N=\sum_i n_i$ and $\bar y$ the grand mean. The total squared variation separates into between-group and within-group components:

$$SS_{\mathrm{between}}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}n_i(\bar y_i-\bar y)^2,$$
$$SS_{\mathrm{within}}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{n_i}(y_{ij}-\bar y_i)^2,$$
$$SS_{\mathrm{total}}=SS_{\mathrm{between}}+SS_{\mathrm{within}}.$$

F Test

Divide each sum of squares by its degrees of freedom, then compare the two mean squares:

$$F=\frac{SS_{\mathrm{between}}/(k-1)}{SS_{\mathrm{within}}/(N-k)}=\frac{MS_{\mathrm{between}}}{MS_{\mathrm{within}}}.$$

Under the classical assumptions and $H_0$, $F\sim F_{k-1,N-k}$. Reject when $F>F_{k-1,N-k;1-\alpha}$. A large ratio indicates that group means differ more than within-group noise would explain.

Two-Way ANOVA

For fixed factors $A$ and $B$ with $a$ and $b$ levels and $r$ observations per cell, the full model is:

$$Y_{ij\ell}=\mu+\alpha_i+\beta_j+(\alpha\beta)_{ij}+\varepsilon_{ij\ell}.$$

Here $i=1,\ldots,a$, $j=1,\ldots,b$, and $\ell=1,\ldots,r$. The terms $\alpha_i$ and $\beta_j$ are main effects; $(\alpha\beta)_{ij}$ is an interaction coefficient, not a product. Sum-to-zero constraints on each index identify the effects.

Main Effects & Interaction

Main effects compare marginal means, averaging over the other factor's levels. An interaction means that a contrast between levels of one factor changes with the level of the other. For a $2\times3$ design, let $\mu_{ij}=E[Y\mid A=i,B=j]$:

$$\Delta_j=\mu_{2j}-\mu_{1j},\qquad H_{0,AB}:\Delta_1=\Delta_2=\Delta_3.$$

A constant gap gives an additive mean structure. Changing gaps indicate interaction; crossing lines are not required.

Hypotheses & F Tests

For a balanced design with $N=abr$, the variation partitions into:

$$SS_{\mathrm{total}}=SS_A+SS_B+SS_{AB}+SS_{\mathrm{error}}.$$

A dot in a sample-mean subscript denotes averaging over that index. The corresponding sums of squares are:

$$\begin{aligned} SS_A&=br\sum_i(\bar y_{i\cdot\cdot}-\bar y_{\cdot\cdot\cdot})^2,\\ SS_B&=ar\sum_j(\bar y_{\cdot j\cdot}-\bar y_{\cdot\cdot\cdot})^2,\\ SS_{AB}&=r\sum_{i,j}(\bar y_{ij\cdot}-\bar y_{i\cdot\cdot}-\bar y_{\cdot j\cdot}+\bar y_{\cdot\cdot\cdot})^2,\\ SS_{\mathrm{error}}&=\sum_{i,j,\ell}(y_{ij\ell}-\bar y_{ij\cdot})^2. \end{aligned}$$

$$F_Q=\frac{SS_Q/\nu_Q}{SS_{\mathrm{error}}/\nu_{\mathrm{error}}}\sim F_{\nu_Q,\nu_{\mathrm{error}}}\quad\text{under }H_{0,Q}.$$

Here $\nu$ denotes degrees of freedom. In a $2\times3$ design, the numerator degrees of freedom are 1, 2 and 2 for $A$, $B$ and $AB$, respectively; the residual has $6(r-1)$.

Three-Way ANOVA

Adding a third fixed factor $C$ with $c$ levels gives three main effects, three two-way interactions and one three-way interaction:

$$\begin{aligned} Y_{ijk\ell}={}&\mu+\alpha_i+\beta_j+\gamma_k\\ &+(\alpha\beta)_{ij}+(\alpha\gamma)_{ik}+(\beta\gamma)_{jk}\\ &+(\alpha\beta\gamma)_{ijk}+\varepsilon_{ijk\ell}. \end{aligned}$$

The $ABC$ interaction means that an $AB$ interaction changes across levels of $C$, equivalently that an $AC$ interaction changes across $B$ or a $BC$ interaction across $A$. A nonzero three-way interaction does not require every lower-order effect averaged over other factors to be nonzero.

For a balanced, fully crossed design with $N=abcr$ and sum-to-zero constraints along each index:

$$\begin{aligned} SS_{\mathrm{total}}={}&SS_A+SS_B+SS_C\\ &+SS_{AB}+SS_{AC}+SS_{BC}\\ &+SS_{ABC}+SS_{\mathrm{error}}. \end{aligned}$$

Assumptions

The F ratios above assume fixed factors and independent errors $\varepsilon\sim\mathcal N(0,\sigma^2)$ with a common variance across cells. Welch's one-way ANOVA relaxes the common-variance assumption for a single factor. Repeated observations on the same unit, nested factors or random factors require a model and error structure appropriate to that design.

Replication: With one observation per cell, a full factorial model including all interactions has zero residual degrees of freedom. Testing all those terms against within-cell error requires replication; omitting interactions to obtain residual degrees of freedom imposes additional assumptions.

Unequal cell sizes: The factorial sums-of-squares decompositions given above assume equal cell sizes. With unequal counts, specify the contrasts and averaging weights being tested; sequential sums of squares depend on the order in which terms enter the model.

Interpretation & Follow-up Comparisons

When interactions are present, marginal main effects can conceal conditional differences. Examine simple effects, such as contrasts of $A$ within a fixed level of $B$. With a three-way interaction, examine the relevant two-way interactions within levels of the third factor. Retain the associated lower-order terms when interpreting a higher-order interaction.

Rejection does not identify every differing pair or quantify practical importance. Report the relevant contrasts and their uncertainty, using a multiplicity adjustment for the chosen family. Tukey's method is appropriate for all pairwise comparisons of one-way group means under the classical model.

ANOVA alone does not establish causality; causal interpretation requires an appropriate design or identification strategy.
