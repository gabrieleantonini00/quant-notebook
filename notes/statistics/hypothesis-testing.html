Hypothesis Testing & Confidence Intervals

Hypotheses, Size & Power

A test specifies a null hypothesis $H_0$ and an alternative $H_1$ before observing the decision statistic. A level-$\alpha$ test controls the probability of rejecting a true null:

$$\sup_{\theta\in\Theta_0}P_\theta(\text{reject }H_0)\le\alpha.$$

A Type I error rejects a true null; a Type II error fails to reject a false null. Power is $P_\theta(\text{reject }H_0)$ under an alternative and generally depends on the effect size, sampling variability and sample size. Failing to reject does not establish that the null is true.

Test Statistics

For $H_0:\theta=\theta_0$ against $H_1:\theta\ne\theta_0$, an asymptotically normal estimator and a consistent standard error give the standardised Wald statistic:

$$W=\frac{\hat\theta-\theta_0}{\widehat{\operatorname{SE}}(\hat\theta)}\xrightarrow{d}\mathcal N(0,1)\quad\text{under }H_0.$$

Here $z_p=\Phi^{-1}(p)$, where $\Phi$ is the standard normal CDF. The two-sided rejection rule is $|W|>z_{1-\alpha/2}$. For a one-sided upper alternative, use $W>z_{1-\alpha}$; the direction must be specified in advance.

p-Values & Decisions

A $p$-value is the probability, under the null model, of a statistic at least as extreme as the observed one in the direction specified by the alternative. For the two-sided normal approximation:

$$p=2\left[1-\Phi\!\left(|W_{\mathrm{obs}}|\right)\right].$$

Reject at level $\alpha$ when $p\le\alpha$. This is not the posterior probability of $H_0$, and statistical significance does not measure the size or practical importance of an effect.

Confidence Intervals

Inverting the two-sided Wald test gives an asymptotic $(1-\alpha)$ confidence interval:

$$\hat\theta\pm z_{1-\alpha/2}\,\widehat{\operatorname{SE}}(\hat\theta).$$

The procedure covers the fixed true parameter in a proportion approaching $1-\alpha$ of repeated samples under its assumptions. It does not assign probability $1-\alpha$ to the parameter lying in a particular realised interval. A value excluded by the interval is rejected by the corresponding two-sided test.

Exact & Asymptotic Inference

For $n\ge2$ i.i.d. normal observations with $\sigma^2>0$, the standardised sample mean has an exact normal distribution when $\sigma$ is known, and an exact Student $t$ distribution when it is estimated by the sample standard deviation $S$:

$$Z=\frac{\bar X-\mu_0}{\sigma/\sqrt n}\sim\mathcal N(0,1),\qquad T=\frac{\bar X-\mu_0}{S/\sqrt n}\sim t_{n-1}\quad\text{under }H_0.$$

With unknown variance, the corresponding exact normal-sample interval is $\bar X\pm t_{n-1,1-\alpha/2}S/\sqrt n$. Without normality, finite positive variance and the CLT can justify a large-sample normal approximation. There is no universal sample-size threshold that guarantees its accuracy.
