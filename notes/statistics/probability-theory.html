Probability & Events

Events & Set Operations

Let $\Omega$ denote the sample space, the exhaustive set of all mutually exclusive elementary outcomes $\omega$. An event $A$ is a measurable subset of $\Omega$ ($A \subseteq \Omega$). The algebra of events is governed by set operations:

  • Union ($A \cup B$): The event that occurs if at least one of the events occurs: $$A \cup B = \{\omega \in \Omega : \omega \in A \lor \omega \in B\}$$
  • Intersection ($A \cap B$ or $AB$): The event that occurs if both events occur simultaneously: $$A \cap B = \{\omega \in \Omega : \omega \in A \land \omega \in B\}$$
  • Complement ($A^c$ or $\bar{A}$): The event that $A$ does not occur: $$A^c = \{\omega \in \Omega : \omega \notin A\} = \Omega \setminus A$$
  • Set Difference ($A \setminus B$): The occurrence of $A$ without the occurrence of $B$: $$A \setminus B = A \cap B^c = \{\omega \in \Omega : \omega \in A \land \omega \notin B\}$$
  • Disjoint / Mutually Exclusive Events: Two events $A$ and $B$ cannot co-occur if: $$A \cap B = \emptyset$$

De Morgan's Laws

De Morgan's duality laws state that the complement of a union is the intersection of the complements, and vice versa. As set identities, for an arbitrary collection of events $\{A_i\}_{i \in I}$:

$$\left( \bigcup_{i \in I} A_i \right)^c = \bigcap_{i \in I} A_i^c \qquad \text{and} \qquad \left( \bigcap_{i \in I} A_i \right)^c = \bigcup_{i \in I} A_i^c$$

Probability Spaces & Axioms

A formal probability space is defined by the measurable triplet $(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, \mathbb{P})$, where $\mathcal{F}$ is a $\sigma$-algebra of events (subsets of $\Omega$ closed under complements and countable unions) and $\mathbb{P}: \mathcal{F} \to [0, 1]$ is a probability measure satisfying Kolmogorov's Three Axioms:

Axiom 1

Non-Negativity

For every event $A \in \mathcal{F}$:
$$\mathbb{P}(A) \ge 0$$

Axiom 2

Normalization

The certain event has unit mass:
$$\mathbb{P}(\Omega) = 1$$

Axiom 3

Countable Additivity

For any sequence of pairwise disjoint events ($A_i \cap A_j = \emptyset, \forall i \ne j$):
$$\mathbb{P}\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^\infty A_i\right) = \sum_{i=1}^\infty \mathbb{P}(A_i)$$

Probability Rules

Property 1 • Probability of the Complement

$$\mathbb{P}(A^c) = 1 - \mathbb{P}(A)$$

Proof: Notice that $\Omega = A \cup A^c$ and $A \cap A^c = \emptyset$. By Axiom 3 (finite additivity), $\mathbb{P}(\Omega) = \mathbb{P}(A) + \mathbb{P}(A^c)$. Applying Axiom 2 ($\mathbb{P}(\Omega) = 1$), we obtain $1 = \mathbb{P}(A) + \mathbb{P}(A^c)$, which rearranges to $\mathbb{P}(A^c) = 1 - \mathbb{P}(A)$. $\blacksquare$

Property 2 • Probability of the Impossible Event

$$\mathbb{P}(\emptyset) = 0$$

Proof: Since $\emptyset = \Omega^c$, by Property 1 we have $\mathbb{P}(\emptyset) = 1 - \mathbb{P}(\Omega)$. Substituting Axiom 2 yields $\mathbb{P}(\emptyset) = 1 - 1 = 0$. $\blacksquare$

Property 3 • Monotonicity

$$\text{If } A \subseteq B \implies \mathbb{P}(A) \le \mathbb{P}(B)$$

Proof: Any set $B$ containing $A$ can be decomposed into disjoint subsets $B = A \cup (B \setminus A) = A \cup (B \cap A^c)$, where $A \cap (B \cap A^c) = \emptyset$. By additivity, $\mathbb{P}(B) = \mathbb{P}(A) + \mathbb{P}(B \cap A^c)$. By Axiom 1, $\mathbb{P}(B \cap A^c) \ge 0$, therefore $\mathbb{P}(B) \ge \mathbb{P}(A)$. $\blacksquare$

Property 4 • Boundedness

$$0 \le \mathbb{P}(A) \le 1, \quad \forall A \in \mathcal{F}$$

Proof: For any event $A$, $\emptyset \subseteq A \subseteq \Omega$. Applying monotonicity (Property 3) along with Axiom 2 and Property 2 yields $\mathbb{P}(\emptyset) \le \mathbb{P}(A) \le \mathbb{P}(\Omega) \implies 0 \le \mathbb{P}(A) \le 1$. $\blacksquare$

Property 5 • General Addition Rule (Inclusion-Exclusion)

$$\mathbb{P}(A \cup B) = \mathbb{P}(A) + \mathbb{P}(B) - \mathbb{P}(A \cap B)$$

Proof: Decompose $A \cup B$ into disjoint components: $A \cup B = A \cup (B \cap A^c)$. Hence $\mathbb{P}(A \cup B) = \mathbb{P}(A) + \mathbb{P}(B \cap A^c)$. Furthermore, $B = (A \cap B) \cup (B \cap A^c)$, so $\mathbb{P}(B \cap A^c) = \mathbb{P}(B) - \mathbb{P}(A \cap B)$. Substituting yields the identity. $\blacksquare$

Conditional Probability & Independence

Let $B$ be a conditioning event with $\mathbb{P}(B) > 0$. The conditional probability of event $A$ given that event $B$ has occurred is formally defined as:

$$\mathbb{P}(A \mid B) = \frac{\mathbb{P}(A \cap B)}{\mathbb{P}(B)}$$

From this definition follows the fundamental multiplication rule:

$$\mathbb{P}(A \cap B) = \mathbb{P}(B) \cdot \mathbb{P}(A \mid B) = \mathbb{P}(A) \cdot \mathbb{P}(B \mid A)$$

Statistical Independence of Events

Two events $A$ and $B$ are defined as statistically independent (denoted $A \perp\!\!\!\perp B$) if and only if:

$$\mathbb{P}(A \cap B) = \mathbb{P}(A) \cdot \mathbb{P}(B)$$

Equivalently, assuming $\mathbb{P}(B) > 0$, independence implies that conditioning on $B$ provides zero informational update regarding $A$:

$$\mathbb{P}(A \mid B) = \mathbb{P}(A)$$
Independence vs. Mutual Exclusivity
  • Mutually Exclusive ($A \cap B = \emptyset$): If $A$ happens, $B$ cannot happen ($\mathbb{P}(A \cap B) = 0$). For non-trivial events ($\mathbb{P}(A), \mathbb{P}(B) > 0$), mutually exclusive events are dependent.
  • Independent ($\mathbb{P}(A \cap B) = \mathbb{P}(A)\mathbb{P}(B)$): The occurrence of $B$ gives no information about the occurrence of $A$.

Total Probability & Bayes' Theorem

The Law of Total Probability

Let $\{B_1, B_2, \dots, B_k\}$ form a partition of the sample space $\Omega$, meaning:

  1. $B_i \cap B_j = \emptyset, \quad \forall i \ne j$ (mutually disjoint)
  2. $\bigcup_{i=1}^k B_i = \Omega$ (collectively exhaustive)
  3. $\mathbb{P}(B_i) > 0, \quad \forall i$

Then for any event $A \subseteq \Omega$:

$$\mathbb{P}(A) = \sum_{i=1}^k \mathbb{P}(A \cap B_i) = \sum_{i=1}^k \mathbb{P}(B_i) \cdot \mathbb{P}(A \mid B_i)$$

Bayes' Theorem (Inverse Probability)

Bayes' Theorem inverts conditioning, allowing the calculation of the posterior probability of a partition event $B_j$ given observed evidence $A$:

$$\mathbb{P}(B_j \mid A) = \frac{\mathbb{P}(A \mid B_j) \cdot \mathbb{P}(B_j)}{\mathbb{P}(A)} = \frac{\mathbb{P}(A \mid B_j) \cdot \mathbb{P}(B_j)}{\sum_{i=1}^k \mathbb{P}(A \mid B_i) \cdot \mathbb{P}(B_i)}$$
Term Mathematical Notation Probabilistic Interpretation
Prior Probability $\mathbb{P}(B_j)$ Initial belief in hypothesis $B_j$ prior to observing evidence $A$.
Likelihood $\mathbb{P}(A \mid B_j)$ Probability of observing evidence $A$ assuming hypothesis $B_j$ holds true.
Evidence / Marginal $\mathbb{P}(A)$ Total probability of observing evidence $A$ averaged across all states.
Posterior Probability $\mathbb{P}(B_j \mid A)$ Updated belief in hypothesis $B_j$ incorporating the observed evidence $A$.
